\begin{abstract}
Machine learning, NLP included, is said to herd: researchers chase what is hot, and review rewards it. We test this account
on 43,480 ICLR submissions (2017--2026), rejected and withdrawn papers included, with hypotheses registered before
outcomes were read and tested again on 11,099 held-out papers. We separate two routes to conformity: imitation takes
its cue from what others do now, continuity from an accumulated record. The field converged: topics with more
foundation-model papers the year before had fewer new-architecture papers. Hindsight inflates emergence: on the same
papers, a topic map fitted on all years finds 29 emerging topics, one fitted on 2017--2020 only 8. Among topic
newcomers (no author had submitted there), where imitation theory expects herding most, our registered
prediction of follow-on work failed in the opposite direction: their papers more often propose methods and less often
benchmarks, datasets or evaluations, and the reversal replicated. Review shows no detectable premium for crowded
topics. In post hoc tests supported on held-out papers, acceptance is lower far from the team's earlier work and
higher with a topic insider on the team. At ICLR, the conformity we can detect points to a record, not to the crowd.
All findings are associations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning is said to herd. Between 2017 and 2026, yearly submissions in our discovery corpus of the
International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR) grew 40-fold, from 384 to 15,411. The share building on a
foundation model (FM) rose from 0.3\% to 54.4\%, and the share proposing a new architecture fell from 24.5\% to
7.5\%. Keywords rose and fell (Figure~\ref{fig:waves}): generative adversarial networks fell from 8.1\% of submissions
in 2018, and large language models reached 16.7\% in 2026. The wave is also NLP's, whose researchers increasingly
publish, and debut, at ML venues \citep{jurgens2026future}. Critics of machine-learning scholarship point to
incentives that favour short-term success \citep{lipton2019troubling} and to convergence on what suits the field's
hardware, benchmarks and foundation models \citep{hooker2021hardware,dehghani2021benchmark,bommasani2021opportunities}.
In cascade and fashion theories, people take their cue from others
\citep{bikhchandani1992theory,banerjee1992simple,abrahamson1991managerial}. Read as herding, the waves make three
claims: they are fashions, imitators carry them, and following pays at review.

Convergence, however, does not require copying. Conformity, a pull towards an existing reference point that need not
make papers identical, has two routes that the herding account runs together. \emph{Imitation} takes its reference
point from what others are doing, or are rewarded for, now: popularity, momentum, recent acceptance.
\emph{Continuity} takes it from an accumulated record: the team's own past work, or a topic's past work carried by a
team member who has submitted in it. \citet{weisberg2009epistemic} model ``followers'', drawn to approaches others have
already explored, apart from agents who ignore others' findings and from ``mavericks''. Later work models herding in
science as risk-avoiding clustering \citep{weisberg2013modeling}, which either route could produce. The route
matters: imitation can discard private information and leave a fragile consensus
\citep{banerjee1992simple,bikhchandani1992theory}, while continuity keeps work near existing competence, with
short-run gains and long-run costs \citep{march1991exploration}.

We call work that conforms by continuity \emph{anchored}.\footnote{We use `anchored' for work that conforms by
continuity, a position relative to a record; it is not the anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic
\citep{tversky1974judgment}, the sense in which writing on herding in science uses the word
\citep{baddeley2015herding}.} Our thesis is that the conformity we can detect at ICLR is anchored to
a record, not herded by the crowd. The field did converge on a shared package (A), as both routes predict. But where the
routes part, at entry to a topic and at review, the evidence points away from imitation. We expected a herd too: before any outcome was read, we registered that topic newcomers would write fewer method
papers and more benchmark and evaluation papers. The prediction failed in the opposite direction.

Prior work relates ICLR acceptance to prestige, process and research area
\citep{tran2020open,ibrahim2026bias,jung2025what,wang2023what,xu2026reviewer} and describes which ICLR papers start
topics, who enters LLM research and how far researchers stay from their own past work
\citep{huang2026catalyst,movva2024topics,bao2025persistence}. Moving far from one's own record goes with fewer citations
and lower publication success \citep{hill2025pivot}, and field histories read trends from topic models fitted on the whole
period \citep{hall2008studying,anderson2012towards}. To our knowledge, among the works we checked, no study tests all
three claims of the herding account within one submission population, rejected and withdrawn papers included, with
registered hypotheses tested again on held-out papers, as \citet{ibrahim2026bias} did for a prestige gap.

We test the account on 43,480 ICLR submissions, 2017--2026. Hypotheses were registered before any full-corpus label
existed, tested once in a frozen run, and tested again on 11,099 \emph{held-out} papers: a pre-registered
out-of-sample replication, not a certified untouched holdout. Every result carries an evidence tier: \textbf{A}
registered and replicated; \textbf{B} post hoc, then supported by a declared held-out test; \textbf{C} registered,
inconclusive or not replicated; \textbf{D} discovery only; \textbf{E} descriptive. All results are associations.

For theories of herding and fashion, we contribute a registered test of imitation at entry, where its prediction
is sharpest, read post hoc against continuity, a route long separated from it in theory
\citep{levitt1988organizational,weisberg2009epistemic}. The reversal replicated (A), and
teams new to ICLR come later to the package (E). For the science of science, acceptance goes with a paper's distance
from its own authors' record (B), in line with the pivot penalty \citep{hill2025pivot}, while crowded topics carry
no detectable premium (C). For computational histories of NLP and ML, hindsight inflates
emergence: on the same papers, an all-years topic map finds 29 emerging topics and an early map 8 (E). At ICLR, the
conformity we can detect points to a record, not to the crowd.

\section{Two Routes to Conformity}
\label{sec:theory}

Conformity alone does not reveal its reference point. Imitation makes teams alike; continuity keeps each team, and
so the field, near its past.

\paragraph{Imitation.}
We measure frequency imitation, through crowding, hot topics, rising keywords and FM adoption, and outcome imitation,
through topics with high past acceptance, but not trait imitation \citep{haunschild1997modes}. Uncertainty breeds
mimicry \citep{dimaggio1983iron} and cascades \citep{bikhchandani1992theory}, so topic entrants are predicted to
imitate most. They would flock to hot topics, take up a rising package fast, and build on what others proposed by
evaluating or benchmarking it: the follow-on work we registered. Reputational herding pays when evaluators judge
people relative to their peers \citep{scharfstein1990herd}, and bandwagons can spread without reward
\citep{abrahamson1993institutional}.

\paragraph{Continuity.}
Search stays near existing competence \citep{march1991exploration,stuart1996local}, and absorptive capacity depends on
prior related knowledge \citep{cohen1990absorptive}. Teams without a record would thus take up a new package slowly,
against the cascade prediction. At entry, continuity predicts that incumbents consolidate the topic's
record. Producer-side studies find that fresh teams and younger researchers try new ideas
\citep{zeng2021fresh,packalen2019age}; on such \emph{outsider accounts}, entrants bring other work. In
\citeauthor{bourdieu1975specificity}'s field \citeyearpar{bourdieu1975specificity}, ``the dominant are committed to
conservation strategies'' and journals ``consecrate productions faithful to the principles of official science''.
Official science, like tradition \citep{foster2015tradition}, is a third reference point, others' accumulated work,
which both routes approach, so distance from it cannot separate them. We measure continuity by where a paper's content
sits relative to its team's record and, more weakly, by a topic insider on the team. An insider is a record possessed,
which cumulative advantage \citep{merton1968matthew} and, where authors are identifiable, visible status
\citep{ibrahim2026bias} predict too.

\paragraph{Timing and the field level.}
Timing separates rewarded imitation of what is popular, under which late riders do as well as early ones, from
priority, under which the premium falls as a wave matures \citep{merton1957priorities,strevens2003role}. A package
bandwagon \citep{fujimura1988molecular}, lock-in \citep{arthur1989competing} and efficient choice
\citep{sutton2019bitter} all predict convergence on a package, so only entry and the gate can tell the routes apart.

\paragraph{Gap.}
To our knowledge, among the works we checked, imitation and continuity have not been tested against each other at
entry, or side by side at the review gate. A recent review of topic choice names social context and path dependence
as drivers and leaves entry costs open \citep{xiang2025research}. We use the Weisberg--Muldoon taxonomy, not its
welfare conclusions, which are disputed \citep{alexander2015epistemic}; our terms, adopted after the tests ran,
label papers' positions, not motives.

\section{Data and Design}
\label{sec:design}

The tests need every submission, past-only predictors and sealed outcomes.

\paragraph{Corpus.}
The corpus is our own dated acquisitions of official OpenReview records (Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Acceptance
tests use 32,190 decided discovery papers, 40.1\% of them accepted, and 8,185 decided held-out papers; withdrawn
papers are left out.

\paragraph{Instruments.}
A \emph{topic} is one of 100 spherical k-means clusters of SPECTER2 embeddings of title and abstract
\citep{cohan2020specter,singh2023scirepeval}, fitted on all years unless another map is named. An LLM with a fixed
codebook gives each paper one primary contribution type. A \emph{method paper} includes new architectures, a
\emph{measurement paper} is a dataset/benchmark or evaluation paper, and an \emph{FM paper} uses or adapts a large
pre-trained model. A blind second pass agreed at $\kappa$ 0.93: consistency between LLM labellings, not human-level
validity. In a \emph{newcomer paper}, no author ID has an earlier ICLR submission, with any outcome, in the paper's
topic \citep[a topic-relative definition, as in][]{biliotti2024breaking}; otherwise it is an \emph{incumbent paper}.
A \emph{first-time team} has no earlier ICLR submission at all; a \emph{returning team} has one. A \emph{returning
topic newcomer} paper is a newcomer paper with an author who has one in another topic, and a \emph{topic insider} is
an author with one in the paper's topic.

\paragraph{Two distances.}
Both use the centred SPECTER2 space. Our closeness measure follows \citet{bao2025persistence}, who use SPECTER2
similarity to a researcher's own earlier papers. \Fmax{} is the highest cosine between the paper and the mean of an
author's earlier submissions, taken over \emph{experienced authors}, those with an earlier ICLR submission since 2021
(exclusions in Appendix~\ref{app:measure}). \emph{Distance from earlier ICLR work} is one minus the mean cosine to the
10 nearest earlier-year submissions, as a percentile within topic \by{} year.

\paragraph{Prospective measurement.}
All predictors use only the past. \emph{Prior FM adoption} is the FM share of a topic's previous-year papers,
\emph{crowding} is the log of one plus the topic's earlier papers, and a \emph{hot topic} is in the top 20\% of prior
growth. Code links leak the outcome into 17.6\% of accepted but 0.5\% of rejected 2023--2026 abstracts
\citep{kaufman2012leakage}; \Tok{} in Table~\ref{tab:main} marks estimates that held after we removed them.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
A registered hypothesis was supported if it had the predicted sign, Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ in its family
\citep{holm1979simple}, and that sign in 75\% of counted variants and in both hindsight variants. These are a
year-balanced map and the 2017--2020 map applied to 2021--2026 papers. Outcomes stayed sealed until one frozen run
\citep{nosek2018preregistration,vandenakker2021preregistration}; post hoc families were declared before they were
run, and held-out tests were registered beforehand and run once. Models are linear probability models with topic and
year fixed effects and topic-clustered errors. Under a null that cuts across topics, the main entry tests rejected
12.6\% and 10.0\% of the time at a nominal 5\%, which a wild cluster bootstrap \citep{cameron2008bootstrap} did not
fix (Appendix~\ref{app:size}). A test not supported and below its 80\%-power minimum detectable effect (MDE80) is
\emph{uninformative}.

\section{The Field: One Package, Less Emergence than Hindsight Shows}
\label{sec:field}

The herding account's first claim is that the waves are fashions: rises that will fall. We ask what survives a
measurement that cannot see the future.

\paragraph{Hindsight inflates emergence.}
Computational histories fit one topic model on the whole archive and read it forward
\citep{hall2008studying,anderson2012towards,klinger2020narrowing}, and dynamic topic models and
BERTopic also fit all years at once \citep{blei2006dynamic,grootendorst2022bertopic}. Some attributes of emergence can be
judged only after the fact \citep{rotolo2015what}, static models can miss small emerging topics
\citep{li2025evaluation}, and a prospective signal has been tested \citep{khanbayov2026topical}. An all-years map
leaks the future into the past, like temporal leakage in prediction \citep{kapoor2023leakage} and hindsight in
judgement \citep{fischhoff1975hindsight}. \emph{Emerging topics} are those whose 2024--2026 share is at least three
times their 2017--2020 share. On the same papers and pipeline, their number depends on the fitting years: 8 on the
2017--2020 map, rising with later fitting years to 29 on all years (Figure~\ref{fig:field}a; E). With 69\% of papers
from 2024--2026, the all-years map is a present-day map. The share of 2024--2026 papers in emerging topics, 22.3\% on
the 2017--2020 map and 40.9\% on the all-years map (20.9--42.2\% across the six maps), is a bracket. The early map
cannot represent topics born after 2020, and the maps differ in fitting corpus as well as window. A registered trend
conclusion also changes with the map (Appendix~\ref{app:hindsight}).

\paragraph{What survives: a package.}
What survives every map is a package. The FM share rose every year from 2018 (Figure~\ref{fig:field}b). Of the 68 most
frequent keywords, 17 instead rose and fell (E). Registered H1d held: where more of a topic's previous-year papers
built on FMs, fewer of its papers propose new architectures. The estimate is $-$7.98\,pp [$-$12.14, $-$3.82] for
adoption from 0 to 100\% in discovery and $-$11.0 [$-$18.1, $-$3.9] held-out (A, direction only). That is about a third
fewer at 50\% adoption than at 10\%, on a base near 9\%. No detectable shift towards empirical or evaluation papers goes with
it ($-$0.63 [$-$3.64, +2.38]; C), and reverse causality is possible. The pattern is consistent with accounts of NLP's
paradigm shifts \citep{gururaja2023build,saphra2024first,pramanick2023diachronic}.

\paragraph{What the field level cannot decide.}
Papers with an independent \emph{same-year twin}, at cosine $\geq$ 0.70, grew with volume but not per possible pair
(E; Table~\ref{tab:main}). Imitation-like spread is plainest in surface form: \emph{branded titles}, a short coined
name and a colon, rose about fourfold (E$^*$). An earlier exploratory analysis, not re-run, found them accepted at the
submitted rate (ratio 1.02; D$^*$). The first claim thus holds only in part: keywords rose and fell, but emergence
depends on the map, and the largest shift, the package, rose every year. It fits both routes, and lock-in
\citep{hooker2021hardware}, so the field level cannot tell imitation from continuity; entry and the gate must.

\section{Entry: Newcomers Propose Rather than Follow}
\label{sec:entry}

The herding account's second claim is that imitators carry the waves: newcomers to a topic follow where others have
led. A randomised trial at ICML detected no herding among reviewers in discussion \citep{stelmakh2023large}, though
co-reviewers' ratings predict rebuttal-phase score changes at ICLR \citep{kargaran2025insights}. We ask about authors
entering a topic, where imitation theory expects herding most and continuity the opposite.

\paragraph{The registered prediction reversed.}
Newcomer papers are 72.9\% of 42,325 papers. Our registered herding prediction, that they would be follow-on work,
failed in the opposite direction, and the reversal replicated (A; Figure~\ref{fig:entry}a). Newcomer papers are more
often method papers, +3.71\,pp [+2.61, +4.80] in discovery and +3.60 [+1.48, +5.71] held-out. They are less often
measurement papers, $-$1.70 [$-$2.46, $-$0.93] in discovery and $-$2.10 [$-$3.35, $-$0.85] held-out (direction only).
All counted variants agree (Table~\ref{tab:main}, note h), and the reversed hypotheses were registered before any
held-out data.

\paragraph{What the reversal means.}
A method paper is a contribution type, not a measure of novelty; an adapter on an LLM counts as method. As three
quarters of ICLR papers are method papers, newcomers could also be imitating the venue's dominant genre, a reading our
registered prediction did not encode. Against it, first-time teams come later to its dominant package, and
newcomer papers sit farther from earlier work. Incumbent papers carry more of a topic's measurement work, the
consolidation continuity expects, but much of that gap is team size, unlike the method
gap (Table~\ref{tab:main}, note g).

\paragraph{Farther from earlier work.}
Newcomer papers also sit farther from earlier ICLR work, in an unregistered comparison added during writing (E).
The gap is +5.90 percentile points [+4.95, +6.85] within topic \by{} year, +5.24 [+4.16, +6.31] among method papers
alone, and +4.33 [+3.44, +5.22] with the authors' own earlier papers excluded from the reference set. About a quarter
of the gap is thus incumbents' closeness to their own work, itself continuity among producers; the rest is distance
from the field's earlier work. The gap is modest on the raw scale (Table~\ref{tab:main},
note i), and distance in SPECTER2 space is not originality. But the
follow-on prediction expects newcomer papers to sit closer to earlier work, and they do not. They also less often have a
same-year twin, $-$3.86 [$-$4.98, $-$2.74] (E; Figure~\ref{fig:entry}a).

\paragraph{Later onto the package and the trend.}
First-time teams come later to both the package and the trend (E; Figure~\ref{fig:entry}a--b). Among 2023--2026
submissions in topics at 5--50\% prior adoption, the FM-paper gap between first-time teams and returning topic
newcomers runs from $-$4.58\,pp [$-$6.55, $-$2.60] to $-$14.71 [$-$19.82, $-$9.61]. Returning topic newcomers do not
differ detectably from incumbents. First-time teams also list \emph{Emerging} keywords, young and rising,
less often than returning teams: $-$3.74 [$-$5.14, $-$2.35]. The package travels with an ICLR record, the order
absorptive capacity predicts \citep{cohen1990absorptive} and the reverse of younger researchers' pull to new ideas
\citep{packalen2019age} if first-time teams are younger. The compute and access a record brings predict the same
order.

\paragraph{What stays open.}
The registered flocking test is inconclusive (C): the incumbent share in hot versus other topics differs by $-$0.73
[$-$2.68, +1.22], pointing the imitation way. Whether entrants crowd into hot topics thus stays open. The method,
measurement and distance gaps speak for continuity only through incumbents. The newcomer side fits outsider accounts
as well, since entering a topic is itself a move away from a team's ICLR record: \Fmax{} correlates $-$0.57 with the
newcomer flag. The FM contrast between topic newcomers with and without an ICLR record does separate continuity from
outsider accounts. At entry, then, every detectable difference runs against imitation: newcomers write less follow-on
work, not more, and the package travels with an ICLR record.

\section{The Gate: What Acceptance Goes With}
\label{sec:gate}

The herding account's third claim is that following pays at review. The negative half of our thesis rests on a
registered test (C), the positive half on post hoc tests supported by declared held-out tests (B).

\paragraph{Crowd, package and past winners.}
The registered crowding test, which predicted a penalty, is uninformative: $-$1.68\,pp per log
unit [$-$3.68, +0.32] in discovery and $-$1.51 [$-$5.20, +2.19] held-out, against an MDE80 of 4.8 (C). Crowded topics
carry no detectable premium (Table~\ref{tab:main}, note b). FM papers, the package itself, are accepted more often,
+4.62\,pp [+2.79, +6.44] (D). That premium does not detectably change with the topic's prior adoption, +0.19 [$-$0.96,
+1.34] per logit unit (D): late adopters fare as early ones do, which fits rewarded imitation and efficient choice
alike. Topics differ in acceptance at ICLR \citep{wang2023what}, even at equal reviewer scores \citep{xu2026reviewer};
we find these edges real but regressing. A \emph{topic edge}, acceptance minus that of all other papers of the same
year, in 2018--2022 predicts the 2024--2026 edge with slope $b$ of 0.38 [0.18, 0.58] in discovery and 0.60 [0.32,
0.88] held-out. With $0<b<1$ (B), a favoured topic keeps part of its edge, not all of it.

\paragraph{Continuity: closeness to the team's own record.}
Holding the author fixed, a paper 0.1 closer in cosine to that author's earlier work goes with +0.55\,pp [+0.21,
+0.90] higher acceptance in discovery and +1.06 [+0.23, +1.89] held-out (B, secondary). Co-author composition may
explain half or more of this. The declared primary test compares teams that all have a record. Among 20,430 decided
2023--2026 papers with an experienced author, the \emph{far jump}, acceptance at the 10th percentile of \Fmax{} minus
that at the median, is $-$5.44 [$-$7.10, $-$3.78] in discovery and $-$6.09 [$-$9.32,
$-$2.86] held-out, with power 0.89 (B). Adjusted acceptance rises with closeness up to the fourth fifth (D;
Figure~\ref{fig:gate}). Larger pivots are less often published \citep{hill2025pivot}; we observe the decision itself,
on every submission, under double-blind review, with a declared held-out test. Unlike distance from an evaluator's
expertise \citep{boudreau2016looking} or reference-based novelty \citep{teplitskiy2022is}, ours runs from a
submission to its own authors' record.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig4_gate.pdf}
  \caption{Adjusted acceptance by fifth of closeness to the team's earlier ICLR work, far to near, 95\%
  intervals (discovery, 2023--2026, papers with an experienced author; D); annotation: declared held-out far-jump test
  (B).}
  \label{fig:gate}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A topic insider: a record possessed.}
At fixed team size and number of experienced authors, one topic insider on the team, against none, goes with
+3.42\,pp [+1.18, +5.66] in discovery and +3.88 [+0.28, +7.47] held-out (B, secondary, direction only). A status
reading \citep{merton1968matthew,ibrahim2026bias} explains this as well as continuity does, and a discovery-only
contrast leans that way. Per head, an insider goes with only +0.58\,pp more than an experienced author from another
topic ($p$~0.16; D).\footnote{Newcomer papers are accepted less often, $-$10.2\,pp [$-$11.8, $-$8.7] (D), but
that gap mostly reflects who writes (Appendix~\ref{app:gate}).} Chaperoned entry into top journals
\citep{sekara2018chaperone} and, on the producer side, expertise that offsets a newcomer disadvantage
\citep{biliotti2024breaking} are parallels.

\paragraph{Two records in one space.}
In the same space, closeness to the field's earlier papers is undetermined (Appendix~\ref{app:tests}, Figure~\ref{fig:gate-tests}). The
least-distant fifth against the middle fifth is $-$3.27 [$-$4.93, $-$1.60] in discovery, the declared direction, but
$-$0.13 [$-$3.50, +3.25] held-out, with power 0.47 (C). The within-author slope for field distance was not supported
either (C). Only the own-record associations held on held-out papers.

\paragraph{Timing: early, weakly.}
Emerging keywords go with higher acceptance than \emph{Mainstream} ones, which are old and flat: +2.67\,pp [+0.76,
+4.58] in discovery and +3.25 [$-$0.18, +6.69] held-out, a two-sided interval that includes 0 (B, weak). Old rising
keywords do about as well (+3.9, nominal; D), so this fits a premium for what is rising as well as priority.
\emph{Waves} are the 17 keywords whose share peaked in 2019--2024. Acceptance after the peak, against up to it, is
$-$5.7 [$-$8.5, $-$2.9] in discovery, not a test, and $-$5.2 [$-$11.1, +0.7] held-out (B, weak). The wave set is
retrospective, and the held-out estimate leans on the six keywords inspected first (without them, $-$1.7, $p$~0.33). A
premium for what is rising would also fall after a peak, but the discovery gap falls before it, which leans towards
priority \citep{merton1957priorities,strevens2003role} (D). Neither test shows the flat premium
that rewarded imitation of what is popular predicts.

\paragraph{What acceptance goes with.}
Of the post hoc patterns taken to held-out papers, the three referenced to a record and the topic-edge regression
held. The timing tests held weakly, and field distance and the federated-learning gap (Table~\ref{tab:main}, row 12)
were not confirmed (Appendix~\ref{app:tests}). Every association that held is referenced to a record or fits imitation
and a rival alike. At the gate, acceptance goes with a team's record, not detectably with the crowd.

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
The conformity we can detect at ICLR points to continuity rather than imitation, though three gate patterns fit
rewarded imitation and a rival alike (Table~\ref{tab:routes}). Conformity here is a pull towards a record, not identical
work: incumbents' papers sit closer to their own earlier work (E), and acceptance goes with closeness to a team's
record (B). Weisberg's risk-avoiding clustering \citep{weisberg2013modeling} appears here as continuity. The
consecration of ``official science'' that Bourdieu describes \citep{bourdieu1975specificity} we detect
only for a team's own line of work.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
Expertise, evaluator conservatism, visible status, cumulative advantage and selection into teams cannot be separated
here \citep{li2017expertise,merton1968matthew,ibrahim2026bias}. Double-blind review rules none of them out, since
preprints can identify authors \citep{bharadhwaj2020deanonymization}. The within-author and far-jump tests add that
acceptance goes with how close the content stays to a record, not only with having one, though close work may itself
reveal its authors. Whether entrants' distant work is taken up less, as in the diversity--innovation paradox
\citep{hofstra2020diversity}, our data cannot say. In the previous shift, researchers who
stayed close to their own record had lower later impact \citep{bao2025persistence}. Acceptance is a short-run
outcome, and acceptance and later citation need not go with the same work
\citep[cf.][]{march1991exploration,rzhetsky2015choosing}, which we cannot test.

\paragraph{Why the distinction matters.}
A field that looks herded may conform by continuity instead; the routes carry different risks: a fragile consensus
for imitation, long-run costs for continuity \citep{bikhchandani1992theory,march1991exploration}. At ICLR the substrate
converged (A), consistent with homogenisation \citep{bommasani2021opportunities}, but no herding of the testable
kinds was detected at entry. For histories of NLP and ML \citep{hall2008studying,anderson2012towards},
an early refit and lagged predictors show cheaply which trend claims survive hindsight.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We tested the herding account of ICLR's FM wave at three points, separating imitation from continuity. The field
converged on a package, with less emergence than hindsight shows; newcomer papers reversed our registered herding
prediction (A); crowded topics carry no detectable premium (C); and acceptance goes with a paper's position relative
to its team's record (B). At ICLR, the conformity we can detect points to a record, not to the crowd: one
venue, associations only, every claim tiered.

\section*{Limitations}

All results are associations at one venue; none is a causal effect of a topic, team or contribution type. The
held-out set is a pre-registered out-of-sample replication, not a certified untouched holdout: it shares
conference-years, reviewer pools and authors with the discovery papers, so it guards against analyst overfitting but
not against year- or reviewer-specific patterns, and supported tests confirm direction, not size. The continuity
results were found post hoc; their held-out tests were declared in advance, but two of the three are among six
secondary tests run one-sided and unadjusted. The imitation--continuity frame was drawn up after the registered and
declared tests had run: it organises those tests rather than generating them, and the continuity predictions in
Table~\ref{tab:main} were written afterwards. Under one simulated null the main entry tests rejected 12.6\% and
10.0\% of the time at a nominal 5\%. Contribution and foundation-model labels are LLM labels checked against LLM
labels, and abstracts are partly camera-ready; links were removed and the text re-embedded for discovery papers only.
Entry status counts ICLR submissions only, and the 2021 switch from e-mail to profile identifiers hides earlier
histories, which pulls entry contrasts towards zero. Withdrawn papers are left out of acceptance tests. Whether
entrants crowd into hot topics is unsettled (the flocking test is inconclusive), and the absence of a premium does not
rule out unrewarded bandwagons. No declared held-out gate test pits imitation against continuity on the same unit; in
discovery, the far-jump and insider contrasts in hot versus other topics are inconclusive ($-$0.35 [$-$3.13, +2.42]
and $-$0.8 [$-$4.9, +3.2]; D). Work that continues a known line may itself reveal its authors, so even the
within-author and far-jump associations do not exclude visible status. The insider and far-jump associations could
be separated from it by asking whether they concentrate where a preprint identifies the authors, as the prestige gap
at ICLR does \citep{ibrahim2026bias}; we did not run that test. The hindsight
comparison is a bracket, because the maps differ in fitting corpus as well as window. The content-distance comparison
was added during writing and is unregistered; about a quarter of its gap reflects incumbents' closeness to their own
earlier work (with that work excluded, the gap is +4.33 [+3.44, +5.22] percentile points), and distance in embedding
space is not originality.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

Author identifiers were used only as keys to compute entry status, closeness and the exclusion of a paper's own
authors' earlier work; no author is named and all results are aggregates. The data are public OpenReview records,
collected in our own dated acquisitions under the platform's terms. No review scores are used. Contribution labels
were produced by an LLM (Claude Haiku) with a fixed codebook; agreement figures compare LLM labellings, not human
judgement.
