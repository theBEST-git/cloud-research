## Judge portfolios

**acl-area-chair**: C1, C2, C6, C7. One question with four complementary identification strategies: what drove the LLM-era title fashion (branding plus register), who carried it, and did peer review select on it?

(1) C1 is the anchor. It asks whether the 2024 branding break is tool, topic, content or people, using a content counterfactual from abstracts with coined names masked and three representations as a bracket. The tool share is reported as a recall-bounded lower bound.

(2) C2 supplies the people channel with the cleanest design. A within-person stayer/entrant shift-share shows communal rather than generational change. My check: about 99% of the +20pp NAME rise from 2023 to 2026 happens within team type, and all-veteran teams moved .169 to .289. A 175-marker scatter contrasts style with topic. C4's within-paper pairs and C15's future-tie placebo become short robustness paragraphs: change is broadcast and acts on new papers.

(3) C6 uses the dataset's unique leverage, the full pool including desk-rejected and withdrawn papers. Stage-wise filters show title fashion is author-side: the accepted/all NAME ratio is 0.95-1.05 in every year, and the break appears in all pools. LLM markers, by contrast, are depleted at each stage, so published-only LLM-usage estimates understate submitters' adoption. C6 absorbs C1-H3 and C3's exit finding.

(4) C7 is the positive control and the NLP hook. In meaning-fixed slots, LLM-favoured variants jump at 2024 while control pairs stay flat. That shows the methods do detect machine influence where it exists, which sharpens C1's conclusion that the form shift is mostly human fashion. Its diffusion claim (H2) must be framed around an explicit recall bound for the union fingerprint and treated as exploratory, with local-model generation limited to a small calibration check (CPU only).

Together they tell one story, which reads as: titles changed through a communal human branding fashion that the LLM era accelerated but did not create, plus a short-lived machine register visible only once meaning is held fixed; peer review neither creates nor amplifies the branding fashion, but it filters out LLM markers.

Abstracts are used meaningfully throughout:
- content counterfactual (C1)
- topic contrast (C2)
- LLM marker atlas (C6)
- fingerprints (C7)

Shared requirements:

Alternate: if C7's recall bound cannot be made informative in a pre-check, swap in C8 (diachronic Zipf abbreviation, born-branded concepts), which is more rigorous but less tied to the story. C13 (namespace crowding) is the alternate if the section is centred purely on branding.

**scisci-reviewer**: C1, C2, C15, C6. These four form one coherent section: a mechanism adjudication for a single large, dated research fashion, the 2024 branding break (NAME: titles 20% to 41%). They take the canonical diffusion and fashion mechanisms and test each with a design that uses submission-stage text from the full submission pool, which published-only work cannot do:
(1) C1 tests functional or content demand and the technology shock (Abrahamson's efficient-choice account vs fashion; Lieberson's warning against external explanations). Content explains at most about 30%, and detectable LLM drafting explains little.
(2) C2 tests demographic replacement vs communal change (Planck's principle per Azoulay et al. 2019; Labov; Ryder; Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al. 2013). Veterans carry about 74% of the rise, and topic change is communal while style has a generational increment.
(3) C15 tests interpersonal contagion vs broadcast (Bass q vs p; Shalizi & Thomas; Aral et al. 2009). In the clean sample, future collaborators predict branding more than past ones (I re-ran this: .118 vs .045), which points to homophily and a field-wide signal.
(4) C6 tests gatekeeper selection (Hirsch 1972; Blumer). The filter on the mainstream fashion is near-neutral (I confirmed accepted/all ratios of 1.00-1.05), while LLM and outsider markers are depleted at every stage, so published-only LLM-adoption estimates are biased downward.

The joint conclusion is a Blumer-style collective selection or broadcast account of a scientific style fashion: a field-wide shift that is not content-driven, not newcomer-driven, not tie-driven and not reviewer-driven. Each leg also delivers at least one positive, quotable result: the C1 counterfactual gap, 'veterans carry it', the published-only bias correction, and homophily over contagion.

The legs share infrastructure: the author panel (C2, C15), the content representations (C1 counterfactual, C15 topic positive control), and the outcome pools (C1 H3 and C6). Abstracts are used substantively as the content variable and as the LLM-marker channel. Feasibility is high because all four have working pilots.

## Candidate summaries

### C1. The 2024 branding break: tool, topic, content or people?

Question: why did 'NAME: subtitle' titles go from 20% (2023) to 40.5% (2026), with a 2024 slope break (OR 1.28/yr; placebo breaks 2021/22 null)? Candidates: LLM drafting (tool), LLM-as-topic (content), new submitters (people), or a shift in how given content is titled (fashion). Hypotheses: H1 content explains at most ~30%: a content-to-form classifier trained on 2021-23 (abstract embeddings with coined name masked; keywords; area) predicts 26% NAME in 2026 vs 40.5% observed (keywords alone: 18%). H2 the tool share is small: the revealed-LLM-adopter divergence vanishes under topic100×year FE (2024 +3.6pp, se 2.4); within-author fingerprint slope ~+1pp. H3 the break is author-side: identical slope change in accepted, rejected and withdrawn pools (OR 1.14-1.16). Operationalisation: segmented logit ITS; DiNardo-Fortin-Lemieux counterfactual with three content representations bracketing the content share; paper-level fingerprints (U+2011, en-dash compounds, em-dash) within author-year; Shapley split into between-topic, within-topic stayers and entrants (links to C2). Identification: placebo training window (2018-20 predicting 2021-23); non-LLM-topic subset; rejected/withdrawn-only replication; 2026 forecast out of sample. Key figure: observed vs counterfactual NAME share 2017-26, gap opening at 2024, with decomposition bars. Risk: ChatGPT, the LLM topic wave, the new area field and 2.5x growth coincide; fingerprints are low-recall and exist only 2025-26, so the tool share is a lower bound.

### C2. Veterans carry the fashion: communal vs generational change in title style

Question: are post-2023 title fashions (NAME:, compound, LLM vocabulary, register verbs) carried by entrants replacing incumbents (generational change, as in online communities) or by the same authors changing (communal change; Labov, Ryder)? Does the channel differ for style and topic? Hypotheses: H1 at least 70% of the 2023-26 NAME rise occurs within stayers (pilot 74%; 81% on non-accepted papers); across 175 strongly changed title words, within-stayer change tracks population change (slope 0.97, r 0.985). H2 topic change is fully communal (LLM topic 104%) while style carries a generational increment (2026 NAME: entrants .475 vs stayers .399; all-new teams +.25 log-odds vs mixed). H3 no tenure gradient in using fresh title words. Operationalisation: profile-ID panel 2021-26 with an email-key replication 2018-20; Kitagawa/Firebaugh shift-share into within-stayer, entry and exit terms; team-type x year logits with team size and area FE; author-FE models with career-age bins. Identification: stayers' and exiters' 2022-23 profiles match (NAME .222 vs .218), plus IPW and an all-exiters-unchanged bound; non-accepted papers for camera-ready; newcomer misclassification bounded by simulating the 22% held-out removal; 2023-25 reported without the 2026 cohort. Key figure: 45-degree scatter of within-stayer vs population change per marker, coloured style/topic. Risk: profile IDs are not persons, tenure is left-censored at 2021, and author order does not establish seniority (no advisor claims).

### C3. Branding without payoff? The selection paradox, strategic exit and the high-variance bet

Question: NAME titles are over-represented among both accepted and withdrawn papers, so over all submissions branding pays nothing (2024-26 success OR 1.03 [0.97, 1.09]). High-variance bet, strategic author types, or camera-ready artefact? Hypotheses: H1 composition: the withdrawal excess is between authors (P(withdrawn | not accepted) +3.65pp cross-section vs -0.9pp within last author). H2 within author, branding is a small location shift (+2.7pp acceptance, -2.1pp withdrawal with author x year FE) and the heteroskedastic ordered-probit scale ratio (1.14 cross-section) falls to ~1. H3 timing: branded papers exit faster (week-1 exits NAME .42/.46 vs rejected .30/.38 in 2025/26) and more often after rejection is final (mixed-case OR 1.24, ALL-CAPS 1.36), a strategic rather than bad-review signature. Operationalisation: outcome ladder (early, rebuttal, late and post-decision withdrawal < reject < accept) dated by 2025-26 modification timestamps; location-scale ordered models; author-FE LPMs; leave-one-out author withdrawal propensity; competing-risks exit hazards; Oster bounds. Identification: withdrawal contrasts use submission-stage text; author FE absorb stable type; the acceptance part is bounded via C5 (current audit NAME net-flip DiD +2.2pp, se ~2.4, comparable to the within-author effect). Key figure: arrows from cross-sectional to within-author coefficients per title form for acceptance, withdrawal and scale. Risk: no review scores; within-author style variation is small and self-selected; exit timing exists for two cycles only.

### C4. Named at birth: within-paper retitling across resubmission, pre-submission and camera-ready

Question: is the branding turn a period effect acting on every titling decision, including retitling existing work, or is style fixed when a paper is conceived? Hypotheses: H1 within-paper NAME change is ~0 after 2024 (-0.007, se 0.022, n=402; 37 adds vs 40 drops), far below population drift (+6-7pp/yr) and new papers by the same last author. H2 asymmetric rebranding after failure: names dropped in ~42% of named resubmissions vs added in ~12% of unnamed ones, above the camera-ready rate. H3 edits do not chase fashion (momentum change -0.002, se 0.012) but swap register verbs and narrow scope. Operationalisation: stacked paper-FE models compared with cross-sectional and last-author-FE year contrasts (Hausman-type test); add/drop transition matrices vs implied steady state; edit taxonomy (300 annotated pairs); typicality change under year-specific title LMs; three stages (arXiv v1 to submission, camera-ready via the audit, resubmission). Identification: paper FE hold topic, team and quality; false-match rate calibrated on accepted predecessors (purity .59/.90/.99 at abstract cosine .80/.85/.90); exact-title families appear excluded from the export, bounding the unconditional change between 0 and the estimate; IPW for resubmitters; pre-2024 placebo transitions. Key figure: year-on-year NAME change for population, within-author new papers and within-paper pairs. Risk: ~400 pairs (~110 strict) only exclude drifts of 6-7pp; motives are unobserved; cross-split pairs need the data owners.

### C5. Snapshot-robust title-outcome inference: camera-ready audit, tipping points and a pre-registered gauntlet

Question: accepted titles are camera-ready snapshots. How much of each title-outcome association could post-decision edits produce, and which claims (colon, length, ALL-CAPS, Title Case, NAME, 'using', momentum) survive a fixed sequence of identification filters and held-out confirmation? Hypotheses: H1 structural features are nearly stable: in the current audit (323 pre-decision arXiv v1 pairs) word-level change is 29.6% accepted vs 23.1% rejected, NAME adds/drops 7/6 vs 1/3. H2 formatting features (ALL-CAPS, sentence case) are not identified: the camera-ready-free withdrawn-vs-rejected ALL-CAPS contrast is null (OR 1.16). H3 composition-driven associations (length, NAME withdrawal) collapse within author; the colon premium survives but erodes (1.30 to 1.12). Operationalisation: per-feature flip matrices by decision with IPW for arXiv coverage (89% vs 56% found); misclassification-corrected logits; Manski bounds and tipping points; an eight-filter gauntlet (year, team, topic, author FE, camera-ready-free outcomes, audit bounds, non-LLM, 2026 separate) with TOST and Holm. Identification: accepted-minus-rejected change DiD removes pre-ICLR retitling; fingerprint dating (the 2025 em-dash surge shows no accepted excess); hashed pre-registration before held-out access. Key figure: tipping-point plot of observed log-ORs against the band audit flip rates could produce, and gauntlet heatmap. Risk: the NAME net-flip DiD (+2.2pp, se ~2.4) cannot yet exclude the ~1.8pp tipping point; casing is unauditable via arXiv; within-author effects have 20-30% held-out power.

### C6. Does peer review amplify or dampen title fashion? Stage-wise filter gains and the bias of published-only studies

Question: treating ICLR as desk screen, author exit, then decision, what does each stage do to fashionable title forms, and how biased would published-only title and LLM-usage studies be? Hypotheses: H1 the net filter on mainstream fashion is near-neutral: NAME gains are exit 0.96-0.98, decision 1.05-1.08, total 1.01-1.04; accepted/all prevalence ratios 1.02-1.03; the 2024 break is reproduced. H2 outsider and LLM markers are depleted at every stage (2026 LLM typography total gain 0.79; ALL-CAPS ratio 0.37), so published-only LLM bounds understate submitters' adoption by 19-48% (rare-marker bound 15.8/12.0/5.8pp all vs 12.8/8.7/3.0pp accepted). H3 only late (post-review) 2026 desk rejections, not early administrative ones, are enriched in LLM-style signals (question titles OR 1.71, new-2026 markers 1.52). Operationalisation: stage gains P(f | passed s)/P(f | entered s) with bootstrap CIs; continuation-ratio logits with year x area FE and team controls; a pre-registered atlas (prevalence, slopes, ITS, entropy, lifecycle peaks, LLM bounds) splitting bias into selection and editing. Identification: desk and exit stages compare submission-stage text (2025-26 timestamps verified); the decision stage and editing term are bounded with the arXiv audit (C5) and a rejected-paper noise floor. Key figure: bias-atlas forest plot of published/all ratios, with selection vs editing split bars. Risk: the headline may be 'mostly unbiased'; ICLR's lenient filter makes biases lower bounds; desk-rejection reasons are unobserved.

### C7. First-hand or second-hand machine style? Estimating LLM-styled titles and their diffusion to non-users

Question: detectors fail on 10-word titles and branding predates ChatGPT. What share of titles is LLM-styled, and did machine-favoured style spread to authors whose papers carry no LLM fingerprint? Hypotheses: H1 in meaning-fixed slots (Enhancing vs Improving) LLM-favoured variants jump at 2024 (OR ~4); control pairs (Towards/Toward, novel/new) do not. H2 diffusion: the fingerprinted vs fingerprint-free gap in the improve slot is 75% vs 46% (2024), 79/61 (2025), 76/75 (2026). H3 register turnover (Enhancing/Unleashing to unified/beyond/bridging) is led by fingerprinted papers and matches newer model vintages. H4 a fashion-adjusted, template-level estimate exceeds word-level bounds (2.7-3.7%) and is ~0 in placebo years 2018-23. Operationalisation: slot inventory (hand-built plus paraphrase clusters); a union fingerprint across model generations (2024 markers, held-out greedy set, 2026 typography); slot-choice logits with pre-trend and topic100 x year FE; positive-unlabeled class-prior estimation with 2026 typographic anchors; reference titles from 2-3 local instruct models of different vintages for 2,000 held-out 2021-22 abstracts. Identification: non-LLM-topic subset; rejected/withdrawn-only replication; a recall bound giving how much undetected LLM use would be needed to close the gap. Key figure: improve-slot new-variant share by year for fingerprinted vs fingerprint-free papers, opening in 2024 and closing by 2026. Risk: fingerprint decay could close the gap artefactually; small local models may not resemble authors' tools; the estimand is LLM-styled, not LLM-drafted.

### C8. Born branded: a diachronic, two-channel test of Zipf's law of abbreviation

Question: when does an ML concept switch from its long form to a bare acronym in titles: through exposure (conventionalization), long-form cost (least effort), or because new concepts arrive branded? Does the compressed channel (title) lead the abstract? Hypotheses: H1 within concept, the title abbreviation-only share rises with log cumulative prior mentions (beta 0.28, se 0.04, concept and year FE; negative between concepts). H2 titles lead abstracts: within concept-year the title share exceeds undefined abstract use by ~6pp, and definition-dropping lags the title switch. H3 born branded: concepts entering ICLR in 2024-26 appear bare in ~40% of first-two-year title mentions (GRPO, RLHF, LoRA) vs 9-12% for earlier cohorts. H4 least effort and ambiguity: two-letter short forms almost never stand alone (-1.99), long-form length +0.71, ambiguity -0.16. Operationalisation: Schwartz-Hearst pairs mined from abstracts (151 with at least 25 definitions), canonicalized and disambiguated; binomial GLMM with concept random slopes; event time from first definition; an LM-surprisal predictability term (Mahowald-style). Identification: concept FE and staggered entry separate exposure from calendar time; pre-2024 and leave-one-family-out (LLM, RL-for-LLM) replications; length-matched cohort contrasts; never-abbreviated multiword controls; 300-item audit. Key figure: bare-acronym share against log cumulative exposure for titles and abstracts, with an entry-cohort inset. Risk: concept identity is messy; the cohort effect may rest on a few acronym families; entering ICLR is not birth.

### C9. Nothing dies, it gets outnumbered or backgrounded: what fashion decline means in a 40x field

Question: when a title fashion 'declines', is it abandoned, diluted by growth, or backgrounded (still in abstracts, no longer in titles)? Hypotheses: H1 at least 80% of share-halving fashions keep counts at or above their peak (title terms 52/62; abstract topics 36/41); true deaths are few and nameable (GAN, few-shot/meta-learning, NAS, adversarial robustness, label noise). H2 backgrounding: title/abstract salience, log(title share / abstract share), falls as concepts mature (deep -0.94 to -1.57; adversarial -0.38 to -1.18) while abstract use persists; within term, salience predicts next-year abstract-share growth (beta +0.045, se 0.012). H3 no loyal core: incumbents leave diluting topics about as fast as entrants avoid them. H4 (secondary) diluting topics carry a post-2024 review cost (-2 to -4pp). Operationalisation: negative binomial counts with log(N) offset and a quadrant classification at three layers (title terms, keywords, abstract clusters k=50/100 with a bge recluster); concepts merged with abbreviation pairs (C8); long-difference panels with placebo leads; author flows from profile IDs. Identification: the title-term layer needs no clustering; per-token normalization for title lengthening; alternative endpoints (2023, 2025); within-topic DiD for H4. Key figure: arrows from each fashion's peak to 2026 in log-share x log-count space, deaths labelled, salience inset. Risk: 'obvious in a growing field'; salience effects are small with T=10; H4 is close to Xu et al. 2026.

### C10. Constructionalization of titles: induced templates, slot productivity and the fossilizing branded frame

Question: can title constructions (skeletons with typed slots) be induced without supervision, and do their lifecycles separate productive snowclones from fossilizing genre frames, as theories of standardization and ceremonial adoption predict (Westphal et al. 1997; Traugott & Trousdale 2013)? Hypotheses: H1 snowclones stay productive (new-head rates: 'X is all you need' 0.88-0.93, 'Beyond X' 0.85) and are not reused by the same authors. 'NAME: a X for Y' fossilizes: rate 0.69 (2019) to 0.11 (2026); within NAME titles of 8-9 words, skeleton q2 falls from 15 to 8.5 (panel check). H2 later adopters' fillers converge on the modal instance with lower filler entropy (standardization). H3 filler diversity predicts growth and survival beyond frequency. H4 tested nulls from pilots: ceremonial decoupling, form-premium decay with prevalence, and Abrahamson's upswing/downswing discourse. Operationalisation: frame lexicon from topic dispersion against abstract clusters (D >= 0.93); POS-typed skeletons; families by edit distance; 600 annotated titles; rarefied new-filler and hapax rates at fixed N=50; decline hazards; split-sample payoff meta-regression on withdrawn/rejected. Identification: fixed-N rarefaction plus a frequency-matched null for mechanical productivity decline; cluster reweighting; withdrawn+rejected replication; BH correction; top constructions pre-registered. Key figure: productivity-frequency phase plot with construction trajectories 2017-26. Risk: heuristic skeletons; small snowclone counts (79 'all you need'); descriptive without the lifecycle model.

### C11. The diversity illusion: title homogenization across abstraction levels, diversity orders and content

Question: are ML titles homogenizing? The answer may flip with the level of abstraction (surface, coinage-collapsed, skeleton, opening move, embedding), the Hill order q, and whether content is held fixed. Goal: a reusable protocol separating style from topic convergence. Hypotheses: H1 rank reversal: raw first-word q2 rises 87 to 246 (n=380), while the NAME-collapsed opener falls 52 to 6 and embedding Vendi diversity rises 61 to 81. H2 homogenization lives at q >= 1 and is genre composition plus within-genre fossilization: among 8-9-word titles skeleton q2 falls 60 to 26, but within non-NAME titles it is roughly flat (33 to 28) while within NAME titles it falls 15 to 8.5 (panel check). H3 structural convergence with semantic divergence: at fixed abstract similarity, title-title similarity falls (-0.065 MiniLM, -0.032 bge, -0.070 name-stripped subtitles). Operationalisation: Hill numbers (q = 0, 0.5, 1, 2, inf) with coverage-based rarefaction; length strata; reweighting to fixed abstract-cluster mixes; 60k within-year pairs regressed on a cubic in abstract similarity; Vendi and compression baselines. Identification: coverage rarefaction for 40x growth; two encoders; bge-512 abstracts (MiniLM truncates 61% of 2026); non-LLM subset; withdrawn+rejected replication. Key figure: Hill-profile panels (2017/20/23/26 by abstraction level) whose curves cross. Risk: 'metrics disagree' may read as commentary unless it overturns a published homogenization claim; semantic divergence may partly be title length.

### C12. What does a title say beyond its abstract? Generative probes of title informativeness

Question: has branding made titles slogans or identifiers? Measures disagree: MiniLM subtitle-abstract cosine fell after 2023 (0.650 to 0.625), retrieval of a paper's own abstract rose (top-1 among 380: 0.852 to 0.928), and flan-t5 surprisal of titles given abstracts fell to 2023 then rose (1.71 to 1.86). Hypotheses: H1 named titles are better identifiers (2026 retrieval 0.944 vs 0.917) because the name recurs in the abstract (92%), but the subtitle is becoming less predictable from the abstract (the 2023 NAME surprisal advantage, 1.56 vs 1.74, is gone by 2026). H2 most of the cosine decline is measurement (truncation, encoder, name tokens) and the remainder is concentrated in 2026. H3 titles less grounded in their abstract are withdrawn more among non-accepted papers. Operationalisation: teacher-forced log p(title | abstract) and PMI under pre-2023-cutoff flan-t5-base and rolling t5-small house-style models; name-masked and subtitle-only variants; same-topic retrieval pools; equal-length abstracts; bge encoder; within-paper deltas from resubmission pairs. Identification: pre-cutoff and rolling models rule out memorization; masking of coined names; topic100 FE and a non-LLM subset; withdrawal given not accepted as the outcome. Key figure: per-year surprisal, PMI, cosine and retrieval under successive controls, split into named and plain titles. Risk: small seq2seq models partly measure their own limits; effects are ~0.01-0.02 cosine; H3 may be null.

### C13. Running out of names: namespace crowding, name formation and citation-free name uptake

Question: with coined names the modal title head (~13k), how are names formed, is the shared namespace saturating faster than chance, and which names do other teams reuse, including names from rejected papers? Hypotheses: H1 collisions with earlier ICLR names rise 1.1% to 5.7% (2021-26), concentrated in short names (12-18% for 3-5 letters) and dictionary-word names (40.6% of 2026 word-names reuse an earlier name; PRISM x14), exceeding a length-conditioned character-LM null. H2 coiners move from transparent initialisms to blends, compounds and word-backronyms; a Chao1 estimate puts the usable word-namespace near 1.5k words, ~40% used. H3 two-year reuse by other teams is 16.1% (accepted), 10.3% (rejected), 9.4% (withdrawn) and dominated by benchmarks. H4 length, pronounceability and wordlikeness predict reuse given content and outcome. Operationalisation: alignment-based formation typology (letters to subtitle or abstract expansion, compound segmentation); collision null from simulated name pools; case-sensitive reuse in later abstracts excluding shared author keys, with semantic homonym filtering; logits with abstract-embedding PCs. Identification: reuse compared within outcome strata (withdrawn and rejected primary) to limit visibility confounding; author-key exclusion; 300 hand-checked matches. Key figure: collision rate by name length against the null band, plus reuse by outcome. Risk: reuse is not influence (the data documentation requires verified person histories for influence claims); generic acronyms; the null is sensitive to the character LM.

### C14. Exemplar cascades: morphological naming lineages from -GAN to -R1

Question: how do landmark artifacts seed naming fashions (-GAN, -BERT, -Former, NeRF, LoRA, Mamba, -Bench, -PO, -R1)? How fast do lineages ignite after external release, who rides them, and do fast risers die fast? Hypotheses: H1 ignition lags shrink with field size: DeepSeek-R1 went from 0 to 60 '-R1' names within one cycle, while LoRA (2021) took off only in 2024-25. H2 Berger-Le Mens coupling for symbolic naming: faster rise predicts faster decline (GAN peak 24 to 6-7; NeRF 10 to 1). H3 status borrowing: derivative names are used more by lower-status teams (gradient b=.068, se .032), and the derivative share falls as names become freestanding brands (24-31% in 2018-19 vs 13.5% in 2026). Operationalisation: morpheme segmentation of ~12.4k lead names; lineage membership when a morpheme of at least 4 characters first appeared as a standalone exemplar; ~25 curated exemplars with arXiv v1 release dates; event time aligned to ICLR deadlines; adoption hazards. Identification: external release dates give a quasi-event study; naming onset is compared with the onset of the topical term (-R1 vs 'reasoning') to separate naming imitation from topical demand; 300 hand-validated short-affix names. ### C15. Contagion or common environment? Title style in the co-authorship network

Question: do title forms spread person to person through collaboration (Bass imitation, q)? Or do correlations reflect homophily and shared topics, implying broadcast diffusion (Bass p: writing tools, feeds, venue norms)? Hypotheses: H1 naive exposure to collaborators' branded titles predicts own branding (b=.144, se .009). H2 this is homophily: in a clean sample excluding the exposing collaborators, past exposure (.045, se .028) is no larger than exposure to future collaborators (.118, se .026). H3 first co-authorship with a heavy brander does not raise branding on later papers without that co-author, relative to not-yet-exposed authors. H4 author-level 'house style' variance shrinks after 2024 as the field-wide component grows. Operationalisation: author-year panel 2021-26; exposure is the share of NAME, mixed-case, sentence case and LLM-marker verbs among t-1 papers of t-1 collaborators that exclude the focal author; own-lag, team-size, LLM-topic, cluster and year controls; future-tie and time-shuffle placebos; topic content as positive control; stacked Callaway-Sant'Anna event study with leads; cross-classified variance partition. ### C16. Payoff-biased social learning: do researchers update title style from outcomes?

Question: do authors adjust title style according to observed payoffs: the field's (success-biased transmission), their own (win-stay/lose-shift), or prevalence alone (conformist bias)? Hypotheses: H1 forms and words whose users were accepted relatively more at t grow more at t+1 only post-2023 (log A/R b=.076, se .022, vs .029 before), but the camera-ready-free withdrawn/rejected signal is null (.011, se .017), pointing to common causes. H2 lose-shift: non-branders adopt NAME next year more after a withdrawal than after a rejection (+4.1pp, se .9; experienced authors +6.6pp); win-stay is weak (+2.2-2.4pp, se 1.5). H3 conformist bias: the first-adoption hazard is more than linear (S-shaped) in lagged field prevalence. Operationalisation: a word/form-year panel with momentum and share controls clustered by word; an author panel P(form at t+1) ~ outcome_t x used_t + experience + LLM topic + year FE excluding resubmissions; discrete-time hazards with a prevalence spline; 2018-20 replication. Identification: the interaction removes the main effect of paper quality; acceptance noise (Cortes & Lawrence 2021) supplies quasi-random variation; withdrawn-vs-rejected is the primary contrast; author FE. Key figure: next-year NAME probability by prior outcome x prior use, plus success-bias coefficients by era. Risk: lose-shift may be composition; effects are small; the two pilots disagree on withdrawn x used (one finds -3.0pp), so coding must be harmonized first.

### C17. Trickle-down, trickle-across or bubble-up? Status and adoption order of new title vocabulary and forms

Question: who initiates title fashions? High-status introduction and later flight (Simmel), or collective selection (Blumer; King's trickle-across)? Does this differ for content innovations and purely stylistic forms? Hypotheses: H1 content trickle-down: within word and year, early adopters of new technical title terms have higher prior-acceptance status (innovators .58 vs laggards .45 percentile; laggards -.073, se .030 with word and year FE). H2 style trickle-across: stylistic words and forms (unlocking, meets, NAME:, casing) show no status gradient (-.02, se .05). H3 elite restraint, not flight: high-status early NAME adopters converge less (41.6% vs 48.8% in 2026) but do not abandon it (retention early x status +.019). H4 no Berger-Heath outgroup avoidance: experienced authors keep words that become newcomer-associated. Operationalisation: 874 title n-grams born 2019-24; Rogers stage from cumulative-use percentile; status = within-year percentile of team prior acceptances and co-authorship centrality at t-1; content vs style from each word's title-only rate (100 words validated); status ~ stage with word and year FE. Identification: the content/style contrast identifies fashion leadership, since stylistic forms carry no functional advantage; staggered word births break stage-year collinearity; status is measured strictly before t; non-accepted rerun. Key figure: adopter-status curves by Rogers category, content vs style. Risk: the style sample is small; status is ICLR-internal (no affiliations); content trickle-down may reflect resources.

### C18. Crowding and distinctiveness: does competition for attention drive branding, and does a distinctive title pay?

Question: as ICLR grew 40x, did authors in crowded neighbourhoods brand more, and does title distinctiveness, net of topic crowding, pay at review? Hypotheses: H1 local abstract-embedding density predicts NAME titles within cluster x year, more strongly after 2024 (+1.4pp per SD before vs +2.4pp after). H2 growth in a cluster's share raises branding (+2.8 to +3.9pp per log share, cluster and year FE). H3 crowding observable in advance (t-1 density) predicts branding beyond contemporaneous density, while a next-year density placebo does not. ### C19. The post-ChatGPT trend-riding premium: content, packaging, or who rides?

Question: title-word momentum predicts acceptance only after ChatGPT (pilot OR 1.05-1.08 per SD). H3 momentum predicts withdrawal (OR 1.056) as much as acceptance, so success over all submissions is ~1.00. ### C21. Claim strength across channels: do titles promise what abstracts support?

### C22. Leading or lagging filter? Text-based prescience of accepted, rejected and withdrawn submissions

Question: does peer review filter out the future? Measured with text alone, are rejected or withdrawn ICLR submissions more prescient of next year's field than accepted ones? Hypotheses: H1 accepted papers are forward-similar and backward-dissimilar (Kelly et al. 2021): abstracts forward OR 1.32 and backward 0.80 (2019-23); titles weaker (1.10/0.88). Within year x topic, forward-minus-backward title similarity is +0.036 SD for accepted vs -0.013 rejected, -0.023 withdrawn and -0.074 desk-rejected. H2 there is no novelty bias at the exit stage: withdrawn and rejected papers do not differ. H3 part of the accepted advantage is reverse causation (accepted work is imitated), so it weakens with pools excluding author overlap, next-year pools of rejected/withdrawn papers only, a t+2 horizon, and papers on arXiv before the decision. Operationalisation: mean cosine to the k=20 nearest neighbours in fixed 2,000-paper t+1 and t-1 pools (MiniLM and bge), titles primary and abstracts secondary; near-duplicate and shared-author exclusion; z-scores within year x topic100; a title n-gram early-use variant. Key figure: forward-minus-backward title similarity by outcome, 2018-25. Risk: reverse causation cannot be removed without citations; effects are 0.05-0.1 SD; 2026 has no t+1 cohort.
